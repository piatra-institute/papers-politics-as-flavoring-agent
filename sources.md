# Sources

## Bibliography

Akerlof, G. A. (1970). The market for lemons: Quality uncertainty and the market mechanism. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 84(3), 488–500. — foundational quality-uncertainty result; background for credence/symbolic quality.

Akerlof, G. A., and Kranton, R. E. (2000). Economics and identity. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 115(3), 715–753. — identity enters utility; makes the sorting (expressive) channel economically standard.

Banet-Weiser, S. (2012). *Authentic™: The Politics of Ambivalence in a Brand Culture*. New York University Press. — brand authenticity and commodity activism; background for §7.

Baumeister, R. F., Bratslavsky, E., Finkenauer, C., and Vohs, K. D. (2001). Bad is stronger than good. *Review of General Psychology*, 5(4), 323–370. — negativity dominance across domains; supports λ_- > λ_+.

Berger, J., and Heath, C. (2007). Where consumers diverge from others: Identity signaling and product domains. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 34(2), 121–134. — identity signaling concentrates in visible/identity goods; background for visibility.

Bhagwat, Y., Warren, N. L., Beck, J. T., and Watson, G. F. (2020). Corporate sociopolitical activism and firm value. *Journal of Marketing*, 84(5), 1–21. — average adverse market reaction to activism; corroborates §6 default.

Bhattacharya, C. B., and Sen, S. (2003). Consumer-company identification. *Journal of Marketing*, 67(2), 76–88. — consumer-company identification; background for loyalty to aligned brands.

Bourdieu, P. (1984). *Distinction: A Social Critique of the Judgement of Taste* (R. Nice, Trans.). Harvard University Press. — taste as social positioning; background for politics-as-distinction.

Chapman, H. A., Kim, D. A., Susskind, J. M., and Anderson, A. K. (2009). In bad taste: Evidence for the oral origins of moral disgust. *Science*, 323(5918), 1222–1226. — moral disgust recruits gustatory disgust; §2 "taste politically dirty."

Christensen, C. M. (1997). *The Innovator's Dilemma: When New Technologies Cause Great Firms to Fail*. Harvard Business School Press. — performance oversupply / good-enough; background for the plateau condition.

Endres, K., and Panagopoulos, C. (2017). Boycotts, buycotts, and political consumerism in America. *Research & Politics*, 4(4), 1–9. — prevalence and correlates of political consumerism; field background.

Escalas, J. E., and Bettman, J. R. (2005). Self-construal, reference groups, and brand meaning. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 32(3), 378–389. — brands as self-construction tools; background for the sorting channel.

Fournier, S. (1998). Consumers and their brands. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 24(4), 343–373. — consumer-brand relationships; background.

Friedman, M. (1999). *Consumer Boycotts: Effecting Change Through the Marketplace and the Media*. Routledge. — boycott as media/reputational lever; §7 discipline-vs-ritual background.

Hainmueller, J., Hiscox, M. J., and Sequeira, S. (2015). Consumer demand for fair trade. *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 97(2), 242–256. — ethical labels move real purchasing; background for credence-quality channel.

Hirschman, A. O. (1970). *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty*. Harvard University Press. — exit vs voice; background for boycott (exit) under low switching cost.

Holt, D. B. (2004). *How Brands Become Icons*. Harvard Business School Press. — cultural branding; background for symbolic quality.

Hydock, C., Paharia, N., and Blair, S. (2020). Should your brand pick a side? *Journal of Marketing Research*, 57(6), 1135–1151. — large-share brands face more advocacy risk; §6 break-even reproduces this.

Iyengar, S., Lelkes, Y., Levendusky, M., Malhotra, N., and Westwood, S. J. (2019). The origins and consequences of affective polarization in the United States. *Annual Review of Political Science*, 22, 129–146. — justifies the bimodal electorate.

Kam, C. D., and Deichert, M. (2020). Boycotting, buycotting, and the psychology of political consumerism. *Journal of Politics*, 82(1), 72–88. — approach/avoidance asymmetry of buycott/boycott.

Keller, K. L. (1993). Conceptualizing, measuring, and managing customer-based brand equity. *Journal of Marketing*, 57(1), 1–22. — brand knowledge as differential response; background for politics entering brand knowledge.

King, B. G. (2008). A political mediation model of corporate response to social movement activism. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 53(3), 395–421. — when boycotts move firms; §7 discipline background.

Klein, J. G., Smith, N. C., and John, A. (2004). Why we boycott. *Journal of Marketing*, 68(3), 92–109. — boycott motivations; §7 background.

Lancaster, K. J. (1966). A new approach to consumer theory. *Journal of Political Economy*, 74(2), 132–157. — goods as bundles of characteristics; background for politics-as-attribute (sorting).

Lee, L., Frederick, S., and Ariely, D. (2006). Try it, you'll like it. *Psychological Science*, 17(12), 1054–1058. — expectation moves experienced liking of identical beer; anchors flavoring channel and λ_+ band.

Micheletti, M. (2003). *Political Virtue and Shopping*. Palgrave Macmillan. — political consumerism as collective action; field framing.

Mukherjee, S., and Althuizen, N. (2020). Brand activism: Does courting controversy help or hurt a brand? *International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 37(4), 772–788. — disagreement hurts more than agreement helps; empirical counterpart of asymmetry.

Muniz, A. M., and O'Guinn, T. C. (2001). Brand community. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 27(4), 412–432. — brand communities as group rituals; §7 background.

Neilson, L. A. (2010). Boycott or buycott? *Journal of Consumer Behaviour*, 9(3), 214–227. — boycotters and buycotters differ; supports separate asymmetric terms.

Nelson, P. (1970). Information and consumer behavior. *Journal of Political Economy*, 78(2), 311–329. — search/experience qualities; background for adding credence/symbolic quality.

Newman, B. J., and Bartels, B. L. (2011). Politics at the checkout line. *Political Research Quarterly*, 64(4), 803–817. — who engages in political consumerism in the US; field background.

Plassmann, H., O'Doherty, J., Shiv, B., and Rangel, A. (2008). Marketing actions can modulate neural representations of experienced pleasantness. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 105(3), 1050–1054. — price cue alters reported and neural pleasantness of identical wine; cornerstone for flavoring.

Reimann, M., Schilke, O., and Thomas, J. S. (2010). Toward an understanding of industry commoditization. *International Journal of Research in Marketing*, 27(2), 188–197. — commoditization as homogeneity, price sensitivity, low switching cost; the plateau condition.

Rozin, P. (1999). The process of moralization. *Psychological Science*, 10(3), 218–221. — preferences become moral convictions recruiting disgust; §2.

Rozin, P., and Royzman, E. B. (2001). Negativity bias, negativity dominance, and contagion. *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 5(4), 296–320. — cornerstone for the contamination asymmetry λ_- > λ_+.

Rozin, P., Millman, L., and Nemeroff, C. (1986). Operation of the laws of sympathetic magic in disgust and other domains. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 50(4), 703–712. — contagion/contamination logic; background for moral taint.

Sen, S., Gürhan-Canli, Z., and Morwitz, V. (2001). Withholding consumption. *Journal of Consumer Research*, 28(3), 399–417. — boycott as social dilemma; §7 background.

Stolle, D., Hooghe, M., and Micheletti, M. (2005). Politics in the supermarket. *International Political Science Review*, 26(3), 245–269. — canonical definition of political consumerism.

Tajfel, H., and Turner, J. C. (1979). An integrative theory of intergroup conflict. In W. G. Austin and S. Worchel (Eds.), *The Social Psychology of Intergroup Relations* (pp. 33–47). Brooks/Cole. — in-group/out-group basis of identity signaling.

Varadarajan, P. R., and Menon, A. (1988). Cause-related marketing. *Journal of Marketing*, 52(3), 58–74. — distinguishes cause marketing from stronger brand activism.

Veblen, T. (1899). *The Theory of the Leisure Class*. Macmillan. — consumption as social signaling; background for symbolic quality.

Vredenburg, J., Kapitan, S., Spry, A., and Kemper, J. A. (2020). Brands taking a stand: Authentic brand activism or woke washing? *Journal of Public Policy & Marketing*, 39(4), 444–460. — authenticity as stance-practice alignment; frames §7.
