# Brief

## Question

When a consumer reports that a politically aligned but otherwise interchangeable good is "better," what is "better" measuring, and can market data ever tell whether politics changed the choice or changed the experience?

## Claim

In categories where functional quality has converged, political meaning differentiates through two channels that are observationally equivalent on market data: sorting (politics changes which good is chosen, leaving its experienced quality intact) and flavoring (politics changes the experienced quality itself). At the quality plateau the labeled market is exactly invariant to the flavoring share, so revealed preference cannot recover it; only a blind-versus-labeled relabeling experiment can. The same plateau condition that hides the mechanism also governs a category-susceptibility threshold, and the negativity-dominance of moral contamination makes brand politicization net-negative for a centered mass-market base while paying only for a base already concentrated at the adopted pole.
